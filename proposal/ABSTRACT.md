# Formal Abstract

## Constraints on Information Localization and Recoverability in Quantum-Gravitational Bounce Models

Black-to-white-hole transition and remnant scenarios motivate a precise question: after a proposed quantum-gravitational transition, from which observer-accessible algebra is information about the infalling state recoverable? We formulate the problem using a reference system \(R\) that purifies an input \(X\) and an isometric extension \(V:X\rightarrow A\otimes B\), where \(A\) denotes a declared radiation sector and \(B\) retained degrees of freedom. For pure \(RAB\), the exact identity \(I(R:A)+I(R:B)=2S(R)\) conserves total reference correlations but permits maximally asymmetric localization. Unitarity alone therefore does not imply equal information partitioning or high-fidelity recovery from radiation.

The model-independent work derives and stress-tests finite-resource bounds. A finite retained dimension gives \(I(R:B)\leq2\min\{S(R),\log_2d_B\}\). For a declared finite-dimensional remnant Hamiltonian \(H_B\) and mean-energy cap \(E\), the Gibbs variational principle replaces \(\log_2d_B\) by the maximum allowed entropy \(S_{\max}(E,H_B)\). For a declared block-diagonal charge-sector state, the retained entropy is bounded by \(H(p)+\sum_qp_q\log_2d_q\). These are correlation bounds, not decoder theorems.

Operational recovery is evaluated through three distinct layers. A maximally mixed-input semidefinite program certifies entanglement fidelity for a declared finite-dimensional channel. A second convex layer computes the channel-wide recovery error \(\inf_{\mathcal R}\|\mathcal R\circ\mathcal N-\operatorname{id}\|_\diamond\) and the complementary distance \(\inf_{\sigma_E}\|\mathcal N^c-\mathcal C_\sigma\|_\diamond\) using unnormalized Choi matrices and the finite-dimensional diamond-norm dual. The resulting implementation reproduces analytic dephasing, depolarizing, identity, and constant-channel standards and verifies the mapped Kretschmann--Schlingemann--Werner inequalities on deterministic channel families. The Bény--Oreshkov worst-case entanglement-fidelity duality remains imported and convention-mapped; its minimax implementation is a separate target.

The first non-holographic geometric scaffold is the Han--Rovelli--Soltani single-asymptotic-region transition metric, evaluated together with the Bianchi et al. white-hole remnant scenario. Because the sources do not provide a microscopic state space, Hamiltonian, radiation coupling, or channel-selection rule, geometry alone does not identify a unique information channel. The admissible model-specific outputs are parameterized constraints, excluded regions, a microscopic completion, or a rigorous underdetermination result. The channel optimizers evaluate declared models but do not derive a channel from geometry. A fixed JT-gravity-plus-bath specification is retained as a separate controlled holographic reconstruction benchmark and is not identified with the remnant model.

Observational work remains gated until a selected model provides a complete emission spectrum, intrinsic duration, population law, propagation model, detector response, background model, and statistical procedure. The project will deliver proof and validity ledgers, reproducible analytic and convex-optimization tests, explicit geometry and symmetry-resource simulations, finite-dimensional recovery certificates, model-specific capacity or underdetermination results, and publication-ready manuscripts that distinguish quantum-information consequences from additional gravitational assumptions.
