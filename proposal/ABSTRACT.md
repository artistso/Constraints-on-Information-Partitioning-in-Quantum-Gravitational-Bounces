# Formal Abstract

## Constraints on Information Localization and Recoverability in Quantum-Gravitational Bounce Models

Black-to-white-hole transition and remnant models motivate a precise question: after a quantum-gravitational transition, where is information about the infalling state recoverable? This project develops a model-independent quantum-information layer and a model-dependent gravitational and phenomenological layer. Let a reference system \(R\) purify an input \(X\), and let an isometry \(V:X\rightarrow A\otimes B\) represent a transition into two output sectors, such as asymptotic radiation and retained degrees of freedom. For the resulting pure state on \(RAB\), the exact identity \(I(R:A)+I(R:B)=2S(R)\) conserves the total reference correlations but does not enforce equal information partitioning. Unitarity alone therefore cannot establish a universal no-filtering theorem.

The primary objective is to derive conditional recoverability bounds under explicit assumptions concerning subsystem accessibility, conservation laws, causal structure, semiclassical validity, remnant Hilbert spaces, and holographic code-subspace reconstruction. We will construct counterexamples and classify admissible channels; derive necessary or sufficient conditions for approximate recovery from radiation using coherent information, decoupling, conditional mutual information, entanglement fidelity, and recovery maps; and evaluate these conditions in selected black-to-white-hole models and in holographic toy models for which a boundary dual is defined.

A separate phenomenology work package will proceed only for models supplying explicit emission spectra, durations, and event-rate prescriptions. It will derive instrument-level forecasts and archival-search strategies without inferring observable burst timescales from the Schwarzschild light-crossing time alone. Analogue-gravity systems are retained only as optional channel-validation benchmarks, not as direct tests of Planck-scale dynamics.

The project will deliver a mathematically validated theorem-and-counterexample set, reproducible symbolic and numerical notebooks, model-specific gravitational constraints, a feasibility-gated observational analysis, and a publication-ready manuscript. Its purpose is to replace analogy-driven claims with a falsifiable program that distinguishes consequences of quantum mechanics from additional assumptions introduced by gravity.
