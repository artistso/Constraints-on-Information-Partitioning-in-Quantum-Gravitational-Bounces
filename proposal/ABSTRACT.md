# Formal Abstract

## Constraints on Information Localization and Recoverability in Quantum-Gravitational Bounce Models

Black-to-white-hole transition and remnant scenarios motivate a precise question: after a proposed quantum-gravitational transition, from which observer-accessible algebra is information about the infalling state recoverable? We formulate the problem using a reference system \(R\) that purifies an input \(X\) and an isometric extension \(V:Xightarrow A\otimes B\), where \(A\) denotes a declared radiation sector and \(B\) retained degrees of freedom. For pure \(RAB\), the exact identity \(I(R:A)+I(R:B)=2S(R)\) conserves total reference correlations but permits maximally asymmetric localization. Unitarity alone therefore does not imply equal information partitioning or high-fidelity recovery from radiation.

The model-independent work derives and stress-tests conditional bounds. A finite retained dimension gives \(I(R:B)\leq2\min\{S(R),\log_2d_B\}\). For a declared finite-dimensional remnant Hamiltonian \(H_B\) and mean-energy cap \(E\), the Gibbs variational principle replaces \(\log_2d_B\) by the maximum allowed entropy \(S_{\max}(E,H_B)\). These are correlation bounds, not decoder theorems. Operational recovery is evaluated separately through Kraus/Stinespring models, environmental decoupling, entanglement fidelity, adversarial channel pairs, and semidefinite programs that optimize recovery and independently optimize complementary-channel fidelity to a constant environment state.

The first non-holographic geometric scaffold is the Han–Rovelli–Soltani single-asymptotic-region transition metric, evaluated together with the Bianchi et al. white-hole remnant scenario. The geometry supplies effective metric functions, horizons, and a free transition duration, but not a microscopic Hilbert space, Hamiltonian, radiation coupling, or quantum channel; missing structure is therefore reported as a parameterized constraint or rigorous underdetermination result rather than invented. A JT-gravity-plus-bath system is retained as a separate controlled holographic reconstruction benchmark and is not identified with the remnant model.

Observational work remains gated until a selected model provides a complete emission spectrum, intrinsic duration, population law, propagation model, and detector response. The project will deliver theorem and validity ledgers, reproducible analytic and convex-optimization tests, explicit geometry simulations, model-specific capacity or underdetermination results, and publication-ready manuscripts that distinguish quantum-information consequences from additional gravitational assumptions.
